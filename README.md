# Green-Dialysis
# Water Management and Valorization in Hemodialysis

[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-yellow.svg)](https://opensource.org/licenses/MIT)

## Overview
This project presents an engineering study and feasibility analysis for managing and valorizing the reject water (concentrate) produced by reverse osmosis (RO) systems in hemodialysis centers. Given the increasing water scarcity and the high consumption of hemodialysis facilities, this work proposes a "Green Dialysis" approach by integrating water reuse, partial recycling, and small-scale energy recovery.

The project includes technical dimensioning, an automated control logic using an ESP32, risk analysis (FMEA/AMDEC), and a financial feasibility study.
This project was developed as part of a Professional Bachelor's internship carried out between April and June 2022.

## Objectives

- Quantify the reject-water flow generated by the RO system
- Identify suitable non-medical reuse applications
- Study partial recycling upstream of the RO system
- Evaluate the feasibility of pressure/hydraulic energy recovery
- Design a prototype monitoring and control system
- Estimate the economic potential of the proposed solutions


## System Architecture
![System Architecture](assets/architecture_diagram.png)

The system is designed with three main zones:
1.  **Pre-treatment:** Existing RO installation.
2.  **Recovery & Storage:** Utilization of residual pressure via micro-hydro generators to power the control system, with battery storage.
3.  **Distribution:** Automated distribution of treated reject water for non-medical purposes (laundry, cooling towers, irrigation) and controlled partial recycling.

## Key Features
*   **Automated Control:** ESP32-based logic for autonomous valve control, conductivity monitoring, and power management.
*   **Risk Management:** Simplified FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) ensuring a "fail-safe" design for hospital integration.
*   **Resource Efficiency:** Potential to recover ~6,000 L/day of water for internal facility use.
*   **Energy Resilience:** Small-scale pressure recovery for backup control system operation.
